An Investigation of Electrostatic Forces of Attraction & Repulsion

Laboratory Report

Introduction

When dissimilar materials are in contact with one another and then separated, the electrostatic charges (ESC) build up and these materials are charged [4]. After the generation of ESC, objects can be categorized as positively charged, negatively charged and neutral depending on their electron equilibrium states. For example, the objects that hold more electrons than positively charged protons is negatively charged [2, 3, 4]. As the objects reach the listed states above, electrostatic forces of attraction and repulsion would occur. According to [1,2], when two objects of different charges come near to each other, they would attract. Additionally, they would repeal if they had the same charges. Moreover, if one gets charged while another one stays neutral, they may attract depending on the nature of the substances.

When two balloons are rubbed against one’s arm or leg, the ESC generated on the balloons would be increased and balloons would get the same charges [1, 4]. So that the form of electrostatic force would be repulsion force if these two balloons are put close to each other. In addition, when a statically charged balloon is put close to the can of neutral state, the balloon can attract and move the can by using the attraction force [1, 2, 4]. However, both the force of attraction and repulsion eventually dissipate as their chargers are released into the surrounding air or nearby objects.

Through these two simple experiments, this investigation will explore the properties of ESC and two types of electrostatic forces. Understanding these properties is of immense importance due to the potential harm that electrostatic forces can cause to high-tech electronic elements, which also makes it a critical concern for various industries [3, 4, 5]. It is highly significant to learn the basic properties of the electrostatic forces. Finally, it will do contribution to finding strategies to minimize the negative effects of electrostatic forces on sensitive equipment.

Objectives

This investigation aims to explore the properties of two forms of electrostatic forces.

The experiment objectives are to confirm that:

1.after statically charging the two balloons, the distance between them will increase over one centimeter.

2.after statically charging the balloon, it can attract and move the can which is placed on the flat surface to 0.5 meter away. 

3.as time goes on, the distance between two balloons and the distance which the can is able to move will return zero and the can will stop in 1 minute.

Methodology

The equipment required for the experiment is as followings: 

Balloons (Latex)

String or thread (3 pieces)

Empty drinks can

Wool material or clothing

Large flat surface (such as a table or floor)

Room with low air flow

Measuring tape or ruler

Stopwatch or timer

To ensure accurate results, all windows and doors in the room were closed, and fans as well as air conditioning systems were turned off to eliminate any potential air flow or interference during the experiment. Additionally, balloons of consistent size, small enough to be held using one hand, were inflated.

Stage1: The whole set-up was constructed by connecting two balloons using three pieces of string as shown in Fig. 1. Ruler was used to measure the distance at their closest point. The piece of material made of wool was employed to rub the balloons to charge them. The stopwatch was utilized to record the time the phenomena lasted.

Once constructed, the initial distance at balloons’ closest was noted. Subsequently, the two balloons were rubbed with the woolen clothing to get charged for ten seconds, after which, they were placed back to their former position. The eventual distance at the closest point was measured and the time before two balloons returned to the original position was also recorded.

In our experiment. the procedures for stage 1 were repeated three times. As for each time, the charging time was increased by 10 seconds.

   Fig. 1: experiment set-up by connecting two balloons with three pieces of strings.

Stage2: Initially, an empty drink can was placed on the surface to evaluate its flatness as shown in Fig.2. A balloon was rubbed with the woolen clothing to get charged for 10 seconds. The ruler was used to measure the distance the can traveled. The stopwatch was utilized to record the charging time and the can’s motion time.

Following these procedures, the charged section of the balloon’s surface was positioned close to the can. By slowly moving the balloon, the can’s shifting was observed, and the stopwatch recorded the time until the can could no longer move. Finally, the distance which the can shifted was measured using a ruler.

Similar to stage 1, the procedures in stage 2 were repeated three times, with each iteration increasing the charging time by 10 seconds.

Discussion

Discussion of Results

Stage 1: The results provided in Table I show that when the two balloons were charged after being rubbed with a garment made of wool for ten seconds, the distance between them changed to 6.8cm from 5.1cm at the initial position. When they went back to the initial state, the time elapsed was 6.3s. By extending the charging time to 20s and 30s, the change in displacement for each trial was 3.1cm and 3.9cm with the motion time of 11.2s and 15.1s respectively. These results align with the predicted outcomes. Objectives 1 and a portion of objective 3 were achieved since the distance between the two charged balloons increased by more than 1cm and the balloons in the three experiments returned to their original positions. Therefore, the experiment showed that by rubbing with the wool-made material, the balloons will move away from each other. Furthermore, it also presented that the repulsion phenomenon will not last for long with all objects reverting to their initial position.

Stage 2: The results from this stage are provided in Table II. When a can was attracted by a balloon, which had been charged for 10s, 20s and 30s each time, it moved distances of 0.63m, 1.21m and 1.79m with time intervals of 6.3s, 11.2s and 15.1s respectively until it came to a stop. These results match the literature [1, 2] and fulfill the objectives 2, 3 and 4 since the moving distances were over 0.5m. Additionally, it eventually came to a stop. It was possible to show that a can can be attracted by a charged balloon.

According to the literature, this phenomenon occurs because after two objects contact and then separate, both will have either more or less electrons [4]. When objects simultaneously exhibit positive or negative electrical properties, such as the two balloons used in the first experiment, they will separate from each other and move in the opposite direction [1, 2]. When one object is charged and the other is electrically neutral, the latter will be attracted by the former, causing the can to move by the attraction of the balloon with charges in the second stage[1, 2].Electrons can dissipate into the air or transfer to other objects, leading to electrically neutral objects in the end, which means the balloon returned to the original position and the can stopped moving[4, 5].

This knowledge of the characteristic of the effect of ESC and that of the electrical forces can help people to pay more attention to them and search strategies, such as avoiding objects with one or both being charged coming into excessively close proximity, to decrease the potential harm caused to both electronic components and human-beings [3, 4, 5]. It could also be positively put into use when there is a need to attract something. For example, the attraction of rubbish such as the cans can help keep a clean living environment.

Limitation and Future Analysis

Although the predicted outcomes were shown for the simple experiments mentioned, it would be beneficial to evaluate these theories more fully. One of the limitations of the tests is that the balloons are only rubbed with the material made of wool, which is limited. Apart from this, the air humidity is not varied and the experiment with the same conditions are conducted only once, which may affect the reliability of the results. Further testing would be conducted using various kinds of material to rub with the balloon to evaluate if the balloons still separate from each other and finally return to their initial positions and if the can is attracted by the charge balloon and then stop moving. Additionally, testing could be conducted to assess how these properties behave in different conditions with varying humidity levels. It would also be interesting to conduct additional trials with the same charging time to avoid errors introduced by randomness. 

Conclusions

The results obtained in the experiment closely match the properties of ESC and electrostatic forces, the repulsion of objects with the same electrical property and the attraction between an uncharged object and a charged one, which is the aim of the experiment. It was successfully shown that two balloons will repel from each other for over 1 cm after rubbing with the wool-made clothes for over 10 seconds. It was also possible to show that a balloon charged for over 10 seconds can attract a can to move over 0.5m. In addition, as time increases, the balloons return to their initial states and the can stops moving. To prove that these results could be replicated in a wide variety of situations, it would be interesting to repeat the experiments using different rubbing material and perform them in varying air humidity levels. Identical trials using consistent charging time interval are required to be done to confirm the accuracy of the experimental conclusions as well.
