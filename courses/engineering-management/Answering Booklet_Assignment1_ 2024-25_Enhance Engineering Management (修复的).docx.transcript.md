👉 Scenario: Your company is designing a new smart home device. You have two material options for the outer casing:

Option A: Recyclable biodegradable plastic, but 30% more expensive.

Option B: Standard ABS plastic, lower cost but not environmentally friendly.

As a DFM engineer, how do you approach this decision? What factors do you consider?

💡 Soft Skills Assessed: Analytical Thinking & Decision-Making, Critical Thinking, Problem Solving, Decision-Making, Clarity of Response (Presentation Skills)

Answer:

choosing the material for the new smart home device is a difficult task. A lot of factors should be taken into consideration. Here are my working processes and factors I consider:

Cost and Future Considerations

Direct Costs: The biodegradable plastic (Option A) is 30% more expensive than ABS, which is a significant increase. But we can alleviate its influence by charging more for the product or improving the value of our brand.

Future Risks: Although ABS maybe safer, using it could become costly later on. Predictably, environmental regulations tighten, sticking with ABS could result in higher costs or even fines.

Manufacturability and Supply Chain

Production Compatibility: It’s important to assess whether biodegradable plastic is suitable for our current manufacturing processes., otherwise we could pay great price to make the necessary adjustments.

Supply Reliability: It is also essential to ensure that the eco-friendly material is reliably available, so that production delays can be avoided.

Environmental Impact and Market Trends

Sustainability: Option A aligns with circular economy principles and supports our commitment to corporate responsibility.

Consumer Demand: As the demand for eco-friendly products grows, Option A could help us take advantage of this trend and potentially command a higher price, attracting more customers.

To sum up, while Option B might save money initially, Option A better matches our long-term goals and brand positioning.

As the lead engineer, how do you decide?

How do you communicate with both teams?

💡 Soft Skills Assessed: Analytical Thinking & Decision-Making, Problem Solving, Risk Management, Communication & Stakeholder Engagement & Leadership, Clarity of Response (Presentation Skills)

As the lead engineer, I would made the following decisions to settle this contradiction:

First, to pinpoint root causes (materials, processes, or design)，I would clearly define and analyze the overheating problem using Quality Control tools such as a Cause-and-Effect (Fishbone) diagram and Pareto analysis.

Second, I plan to evaluate the proposed options. In one hand, the Quality team's suggestion of additional testing provides deeper defect insights but delays immediate solutions. In the other hand, the Manufacturing team's heat sink redesign quickly addresses overheating but involves potential risks and costs. Therefore, I'd use Six Sigma's DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control) methodology to systematically assess these options, balancing technical feasibility, risk, cost-effectiveness, and time-to-market.

Moreover, I would communicate transparently with both teams, ensuring alignment and clarity:

With the Quality team, emphasize how targeted testing aligns with long-term reliability and customer satisfaction, supported by data visualization like Pareto and Control charts.

With the Manufacturing team, explain how design adjustments using Robust Design principles (Taguchi methods) and optimized material choices can effectively reduce variability, minimize overheating risks, and lower overall costs.

My approach also includes conducting regular joint meetings and feedback sessions, utilizing clear visual presentations (flowcharts, diagrams), to maintain effective stakeholder communication, facilitate collaboration, and ensure timely issue resolution.

Question 3 [10/100]

👉 Scenario: Your company is designing a high-efficiency PCB (Printed Circuit Board) for sensors used in connected devices, such as smart home appliances or industrial systems. A supplier informs you of ±10% variability in copper trace thickness.

How would you apply the Taguchi robust design principles to ensure the PCB performs consistently despite this variability?

How would you collaborate with the supplier to minimize the impact of this variability on the final product?

💡 Soft Skills Assessed: Analytical Thinking & Decision-Making, Critical Thinking, Communication & Teamwork, Problem Solving, Clarity of Response (Presentation Skills)

To handle the ±10% variability in copper trace thickness, I would start by clearly identifying key factors affecting PCB performance. Taguchi robust design principles provide a structured method to analyze factors such as trace thickness, materials, environmental conditions, and manufacturing processes.

I propose to conduct targeted experiments using Taguchi's Orthogonal Arrays. This approach helps evaluate each factor's impact on PCB reliability. These experiments could find the best parameter settings to reduce sensitivity to variability, ensuring consistent performance.

Using the Taguchi Quadratic Loss Function could also help measure the cost associated with performance variations. This quantification guides effective decision-making about managing acceptable variability levels.

For supplier collaboration, clear and transparent communication about shared quality goals is vital. Joint audits of manufacturing processes can identify and address variability causes. Sharing detailed experimental results helps suppliers understand how specific process improvements enhance product quality.

This collaborative approach is essential for minimizing variability, improving production quality, and achieving reliable PCB performance.
