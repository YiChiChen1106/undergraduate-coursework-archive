Part 2: Initial Design Report (About 4 minutes)

Our goal is to build a drone that can perform six tasks: take-off, tunnel flying, obstacle avoidance, finding and picking up packages, dropping packages precisely, and sending wireless messages. To achieve these tasks, we used a modular design and combined different sensors for better performance.

The STM32F427 processor is the core of our system. We chose this processor because it effectively handles complex sensor data and drone commands, making sure the drone responds quickly and reliably.

We selected the PIX2.4.8 flight controller due to its advanced sensor integration, helping our drone maintain stable flight in complicated indoor environments. The PX4FLOW optical flow sensor provides precise positioning even without GPS, crucial for accurate indoor navigation, particularly in tight spaces like tunnels.

Our drone is powered by a powerful lithium battery (4S 1300mAh, 95C), chosen for its high energy output and reliable performance during intense maneuvers. The high-torque T-Motor V2306 brushless motors ensure the drone has enough power for quick vertical take-off and responsive movements, essential for obstacle avoidance and precise payload handling. The 20A ESCs efficiently manage the power delivery, contributing to the drone’s agility and responsiveness.

For vision and communication, we chose a high-resolution FPV camera capable of accurately identifying and tracking packages and markers. Additionally, our wireless communication module was selected for its reliability, allowing prompt transmission of messages once tasks are completed.

During our initial testing, we will use a remote controller and receiver mentioned in our initial report. This allows us to manually control and debug the drone, ensuring everything works correctly before moving on to fully autonomous operations.

Part 3: Experimental Progress and Challenges (About 4 minutes)

Since starting, our team has met regularly, set clear objectives, purchased necessary components, and began constructing and testing our drone.

So far, we have successfully built and verified the drone frame, ensuring it fits within the size requirements for tunnel navigation. Additionally, we have started initial software debugging, such as testing flight control data and communication with the ground station.

Despite our progress, we faced several specific issues. Initially, our limited soldering experience led to improper solder joints and damage to several components, resulting in delays and additional expenses. We also faced setbacks due to incorrect specifications of some purchased components, causing us to return and reorder parts, slowing down our schedule.

To address these challenges, we made focused efforts, such as practicing soldering techniques under supervision to improve our technical skills. We now clearly communicate with suppliers, carefully verifying all component specifications to avoid future mismatches. We have also increased the frequency and detail of our team meetings and tests to better coordinate our efforts.

Although our current progress is slightly behind our initial schedule, we have made clear plans to speed up our work significantly in the next two months. We are committed to overcoming these challenges through dedicated effort and teamwork, ensuring we successfully complete all project tasks on time.

Our immediate next steps include detailed experiments with obstacle avoidance, refining mechanical systems for package handling, and enhancing communication reliability, all contributing to a fully integrated drone ready to achieve all tasks effectively.
